    description: Is personality defined by momentary states or by stable traits? We discuss how states are measured and how they relate to traits.

    description: Variability across and within contexts, split into inconsistency, responsiveness, and rigidity. We compute our own variability from a mini experience sampling study.

    description: How do situations shape personality states, and how does personality shape the situations we seek out? We discuss Matz and Harari (2021) in expert groups.

    description: Personality as a set of situation-specific if-then patterns. We compare our own mini-ESM results with general Big Five findings.

    description: The PersDyn model (Sosnowska et al., 2019) describes people by their attractor state, variability, and attractor strength. We also compare different models of personality and their trade-offs.

  - week: 7
    topic: Personality as a Network
    description: Personality traits as interacting components in a network. We compare our own trait networks and name the clusters we find.

  - week: 8
    topic: Emotion Dynamics
    description: How do personality and daily emotional processes relate (Komulainen et al., 2014)? We discuss whether personality and emotions can be separated meaningfully.

  - week: 9
    topic: Personality Change

  - week: 10
    topic: "Personality Disorders: Narcissism"
    description: How can a dynamic process view explain the emergence of personality disorders? We discuss Wright et al. (2017) and their dynamic model of narcissism.

    description: How well does personality predict job performance, and how do general and work-specific personality differ (Bowling & Burns, 2010)? We discuss what this means for personnel selection.

## Course Overview

Traditional personality research describes people by stable traits. In this seminar, we instead treat personality as a dynamic process that unfolds over time and across situations, and we work with data from our own mini experience sampling studies.
